# Slack Message

Notes, bullets, or a rough draft → Slack-ready internal message for engineering, product, tech leadership, cross-functional, or department-wide audiences in a scale-up tech org (mixed technical and non-technical readers).

## Output

- Slack scanning: short paragraphs, bullets, light headings. Calm, deliberate, professional. Keep the user's intent, ask, and audience unless asked to reposition.
- Substantial messages: Context → What is changing → Why → Impact / expectations → Next steps. Compress for short announcements or direct asks.
- Lead with why it's sent and the business/operational intent. Name the accountable role (CTO, VP Engineering, Engineering/Product/Technology Leadership) instead of "we decided" / "it was agreed". State who is affected, what changes, what stays, what action is expected; translate policy into day-to-day meaning; end with next steps, timing, docs, or contacts.
- Frame standards, governance, capacity, compliance, or ops changes as delivery predictability, sustainability, risk reduction, quality, or customer outcomes.
- Prefer: "Following up from…", "To support…", "Going forward…", "This means…", "Teams should…", "The expectation is…". Avoid: "Just a quick note…", "Exciting announcement…", "Please be advised…", "As everyone knows…", "Hope this makes sense.", "If helpful, I can…" closes (unless the user wants to offer follow-up), commanding openings ("Need you to…", "You should…").
- Direct business phrasing ("running short on suitable candidates", "risk of rollout stall") over vague ("may impact progress").
- Default close: "Thanks in advance. Please reply here if you have a preference or concerns." unless a stronger ask is requested.

## Sensitive messages

Line managers, leadership, escalations, blockers, policy friction, politically sensitive topics:

- Open with context and operational intent ("To keep <goal> on track, I want to align on…") — not "We have a problem with…" / "You need to…".
- Frame the issue as a delivery, coordination, policy, or operational constraint before any team or role ("The current dependency is creating a rollout constraint…" — not "Team X is blocking this…"). System-first over person-first blame; name the consequence before any failure or resistance.
- Alignment/confirmation/decision asks ("Can you confirm whether…", "Could you decide between…") — not directives ("Please do X by Friday") unless explicitly wanted.
- Explain why it matters before or with the ask; consequence phrasing ("Without this decision, we risk…") — not status-heavy ("This is unacceptable").
- Keep the substance; clear and direct, no vague hedging. Direct attribution only when the user asks for sharper ownership.

## Decision requests

Escalation or blocker needing leadership action: ask for the decision directly (don't offer to prepare more material). Lead with the decision needed → 2–5 compact progress bullets → blocker in operational terms → options only if in the user's input → consequence of delay + soft in-thread reply request.

## Example

Before: `We need a decision from line managers now. The current behavior is blocking rollout and the team cannot keep waiting. Please confirm today.`

After: `To keep the rollout on track, I want to align on the decision path for manager approval. The current dependency is slowing rollout planning for the next batch. Can you confirm today whether we should proceed with option A or B? Without that decision, the next rollout window is at risk.`
